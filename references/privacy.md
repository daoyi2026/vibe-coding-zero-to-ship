# Privacy

Privacy becomes a product requirement when the product collects information about real people.

## Ask internally

- What data is collected?
- Why is it needed?
- Who can see it?
- Is any of it public?
- Which third parties receive it?
- When and how can it be deleted?

Examples include names, email, phone, location, photos, messages, uploaded files, personal notes, identifiers, usage history, and sensitive information.

## Principles

- Minimize collection: do not gather data merely because it might be useful later.
- Make visibility match user expectations.
- Do not treat AI APIs, analytics, email providers, storage providers, or payment providers as invisible if sending data to them materially affects privacy.
- If users can create accounts/content, define meaningful deletion behavior.
- Avoid promising deletion or privacy behavior that the implementation does not actually provide.

For higher-risk or regulated contexts, surface that professional/legal review may be appropriate rather than pretending this checklist proves compliance.
